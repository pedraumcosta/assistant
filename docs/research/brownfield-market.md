## Part 2 — Demand and economics

- **DORA, "ROI of AI-assisted Software Development" v.2026.1** (report; the figure is cited from Stanford research, not measured by DORA): "a 35–40% productivity gain on simple, greenfield tasks, its impact on complex, legacy brownfield code is often 10% or less" [P] ([PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/dora-roi-of-ai-assisted-software-development-2026.pdf), p.35; release date 2026-04-22 is [S]). The "nearly 100,000 developers" sample size appears only in secondary write-ups [S]; the PDF says "tens of thousands of engineers" [P].
- **McKinsey, "AI for IT modernization", December 2024** (consultancy experience, not a controlled study): tech debt "in many cases up to 40 to 50 percent of total investment spend"; "a 40 to 50 percent acceleration in tech modernization timelines and a 40 percent reduction in costs"; "as much as 70 percent of the software used by Fortune 500 companies was developed 20 or more years ago"; traditional programmes "five to seven years" [P-mirror] ([PDF mirror](https://www.hkdca.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/ai-it-modernization-mckinsey.pdf); mckinsey.com returned 403).
- **Market size (forecasts, market-research firms, [S] only):** application-modernisation services USD 28.2bn in 2026 (Grand View), 28.8bn (Straits), 28.40bn (Precedence), 25.62bn (Research Nester). Gartner and Forrester figures: not found (paywalled).
- **Factory "Legacy-Bench"** (vendor-authored benchmark, 2026-04-01): pass rates "from 16.9% to 42.5% across the 12 model-agent combinations" versus ">70%" on SWE-bench Verified; 46% of tasks are COBOL; "Of the 44 tasks that no model solves, 31 are COBOL"; migration scores roughly half of implementation, which is roughly half of bug fixing; "In 97% of failures, the agent believes it has solved the task" [P] ([Factory](https://factory.ai/news/legacy-bench); [repo](https://github.com/factory-ai/legacy-bench)).
- **CodeScene** (vendor claim): "AI agents introduce at least 60% more defects and burn up to 50% more tokens in unhealthy code" [P] ([codescene.com](https://codescene.com/), 2026-10-05).

Reading: demand is real and large, but the independent evidence says agents are weakest exactly where the thesis aims, and the buyers with budget are regulated enterprises that purchase through hyperscalers and integrators.

## How occupied is brownfield

- **Hyperscalers and model vendors own the mainframe, .NET, Java-upgrade and VMware migrations**, and three of them give the tooling away (AWS free agents; Copilot bundled; Google pilots) because the payoff is cloud consumption. Anthropic published its migration method and starter kit for free.
- **Funded startups own** behaviour-verified mainframe rewrite (Mechanical Orchard, Hypercubic), deterministic mass refactoring (Moderne), large-codebase context (Augment, Tabnine/Tricentis, Sourcegraph, CAST, Unblocked) and test generation (Diffblue). Two specialists have already left: Grit sunset into Honeycomb; Bloop pivoted away.
- **Relatively open:** everyday change in non-mainframe legacy systems governed by explicit risk zones; long-tail stacks (VB6, Delphi, PowerBuilder, Oracle Forms, RPG) where only small players operate; independent verification of someone else's migration.

**Best argument that a newcomer cannot win.** The four practices are process, not technology. Each is reproducible as a prompt, skill file or MCP server, and the vendors with models ship them as free guidance. The primary sources show the decisive asset is the customer's own test suite or production traffic, which a tool cannot supply; where scaffolding was measured (Asana, Teleport) it lost to simple prompts. Buyers are regulated enterprises that require references, on-prem deployment and an integrator: three things a team with no customers lacks. Margins sit under rented models, and each model release absorbs more of the harness.

## What a newcomer could plausibly own

Honest answer: **nothing obvious as a horizontal product.** Candidates, with the objection to each:

1. **Independent equivalence verification** ("the auditor, not the migrator"): record/replay and differential testing sold to whoever is being migrated by AWS, IBM or an integrator. Legacy-Bench's 97% false-confidence finding is the best demand signal found. Objection: Dual Run and Imogen exist, and it needs production-data access that only trust earns.
2. **One long-tail stack done end to end** (for example VB6 or Oracle Forms), with corrections accumulated across customers as the asset. Objection: this is choosing a vertical, which the premise excludes, and Kodesage is already there for Oracle Forms.
3. **Zoning and correction-memory as a governance layer** for existing agents. Objection: thin, a feature of Qodo, Unblocked and CodeScene, and contradicted by the Asana and Teleport evidence.

Without picking a stack or a regulated niche and earning the first reference customers by services-heavy work, there is no defensible entry here.
